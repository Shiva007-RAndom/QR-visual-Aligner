1. Mission Overview & Field Spatial Separation

The mission requires an autonomous quadcopter UAV to conduct an end-to-end aerial reconnaissance and precision alignment operation over a 50m × 50m field. To eliminate ambiguity and prevent false self-matching, the operating space is rigorously segmented into two physical zones:

  • Blue Takeoff & Memorization Zone (50m × 10m): Spans Gazebo $Y \in [-30.0, -20.0]$ (ArduPilot North $\in [-30.0, -20.0]$). The quadcopter spawns directly over an initial reference QR code (2m × 2m) at $(N=-25.0, E=0.0)$. The drone ascends to 4.0m altitude, holds steady hover, and memorizes the visual QR module pattern into volatile RAM.
  • Green Search Zone (50m × 40m): Spans Gazebo $Y \in [-20.0, +20.0]$ (ArduPilot North $\in [-20.0, +20.0]$). This zone contains four candidate 2m × 2m QR tiles (`qr_tile_1` to `qr_tile_4`), exactly one of which is an identical twin of the takeoff pattern. The search zone is spatially isolated from the takeoff pad by a 5m safety buffer, guaranteeing that the drone cannot self-detect its takeoff pad during search.
  • Twin Pattern Similarity vs. Barcode Decoding: Real-world field reconnaissance cannot assume perfect barcode integrity (e.g. unencoded patterns, distortion, or surface wear). The vision system converts the QR into a normalized 21 × 21 binary module grid and performs a 4-rotation invariant similarity comparison.
  • Visual Alignment & Steady Hover: Upon detecting the twin pattern (similarity $\ge 88\%$), the drone must arrest forward survey momentum immediately, project the QR's exact world coordinates, visually center the downward optical crosshair over the QR centroid with sub-decimeter precision, and hold a locked hover for a configurable duration (default: 10.0 seconds).

Gazebo World Frame (ENU)

Convention: East-North-Up standard.
• +X: East
• +Y: North
• +Z: Upwards into the sky
World origin $(0,0,0)$ is at field center. Drone spawns at $(X=0, Y=-25)$.

ArduPilot Local Frame (NED)

Convention: North-East-Down aviation standard.

4. Computer Vision & QR Similarity Algorithm

To ensure robustness against lighting, yaw rotation, and non-standard unencoded QR patterns, the vision pipeline implements a four-stage normalized correlation pipeline:

Figure 2: Perspective-rectified computer vision feature extraction and rotation-invariant similarity scoring pipeline.

4-Rotation Invariant Module Grid Correlation Formula

Let $\mathbf{G}_{\text{ref}} \in \{0, 1\}^{21 \times 21}$ be the binary matrix memorized during takeoff. When a candidate QR is detected during the lawnmower survey, its rotation relative to the camera is unknown due to quadcopter yaw variations. The similarity score $S$ is computed across all 4 discrete rotations:

$$S(\mathbf{G}_{\text{ref}}, \mathbf{G}_{\text{cand}}) = \max_{k \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}} \left( \frac{1}{441} \sum_{r=1}^{21} \sum_{c=1}^{21} \mathbb{I}\left( \mathbf{G}_{\text{ref}}[r, c] == \text{rot}_{90}^k(\mathbf{G}_{\text{cand}})[r, c] \right) \right)$$

Where $\mathbb{I}(\cdot)$ is the indicator function returning 1 on match and 0 on mismatch, and $441 = 21 \times 21$ is the total module count. A threshold $S \ge 0.88$ reliably flags a positive match (twin yields $1.00$ / $100\%$) while rejecting all non-twin patterns ($S < 0.60$). Simultaneously, payload text decoding ("AEROTHON_QR_02") runs as a zero-latency parallel verification.

5. Root Cause Analysis: Why Visual Alignment Previously Drifted

In early testing, the telemetry logs reported: [MATCH FOUND!] Candidate QR Similarity: 90.6%, followed by visual alignment logs: [CENTERING] Error: 228.2px -> Adjust N: +0.60m, E: +0.60m. However, the drone continued flying south and eventually landed at $(N=-1.84\text{m}, E=3.00\text{m})$ on bare grass. This caused the user to remark: "still th visual alignment part is nt good ig / it landed but i think the matcher didn't work".

The Three Interconnected Root Causes of Alignment Failure
  1. MAVLink Mode Rejection: Calling set_mode("BRAKE") via pymavlink's generic helper sent a broadcast command without explicit target system and component IDs (sys=1, comp=1). ArduPilot dropped the packet without error. The drone remained in waypoint navigation mode with $1.5\text{ m/s}$ southward momentum.
  2. Moving-Frame Relative Setpoint Runaway: The old alignment loop attempted to compute incremental adjustments relative to drone.ned. Because the drone was already in high-speed motion southward towards Waypoint 4 ($N=-16.0$), adding $+0.6\text{m}$ to the current position simply dragged the setpoint south along with the drone!
  3. Forced Landing on Timeout: When the alignment timeout elapsed without convergence, the script exited and called drone.land(), putting the drone down wherever it happened to be, creating the illusion that the matcher had failed.

6. Precision Visual Servoing: Mathematical Formulation

To achieve sub-decimeter visual centering, the revised system transitions from relative step nudges to Position-Based Visual Servoing (PBVS) anchored to the world frame.

Step 1: Pinhole Optical Projection Geometry

The downward camera has horizontal field of view $\text{HFOV} = 90^\circ$ ($1.5707963\text{ rad}$) and image dimensions $W = 640\text{ px}, H = 480\text{ px}$, with principal point $(c_x, c_y) = (320, 240)$. At altitude $h = 4.0\text{m}$, the ground resolution scale $S$ is:

$$S = \frac{2 \cdot h \cdot \tan(\text{HFOV} / 2)}{W} = \frac{2 \cdot 4.0 \cdot \tan(45^\circ)}{640} = \frac{8.00\text{ m}}{640\text{ px}} = 0.0125\text{ m/pixel} \quad (1.25\text{ cm/pixel})$$

Step 2: Centroid Error to Body Displacement Translation

Given detected QR centroid $(u, v)$ in pixel coordinates, the translation vector $\begin{bmatrix} \Delta X_{\text{body}} \\ \Delta Y_{\text{body}} \end{bmatrix}$ in the quadcopter's forward-right body frame is:

$$\Delta X_{\text{body}} = -(v - c_y) \cdot S, \qquad \Delta Y_{\text{body}} = +(u - c_x) \cdot S$$

Step 3: Compass Yaw Rotation to World NED Coordinates

Transforming body frame displacements into world North-East coordinates using the quadcopter's measured heading yaw angle $\psi$:

$$\begin{bmatrix} \Delta N \\ \Delta E \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\psi & -\sin\psi \\ \sin\psi & \cos\psi \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \Delta X_{\text{body}} \\ \Delta Y_{\text{body}} \end{bmatrix}$$
Fixed Target World Anchor Principle
Rather than continually adding offsets to a moving drone, the target QR world coordinate is calculated and latched once: $$\mathbf{P}_{\text{target\_ned}} = \begin{bmatrix} N_{\text{drone}} + \Delta N \\ E_{\text{drone}} + \Delta E \\ -h \end{bmatrix}$$ This single calculation establishes an immovable, fixed spatial anchor in the world frame ($N \approx 8.0\text{m}, E \approx -12.0\text{m}$).

7. Multi-Iteration Closed-Loop Visual Servoing Algorithm

Once the quadcopter arrives within 25 cm of the calculated world target, aerodynamic ground effect or slight EKF velocity overshoot may leave a small residual pixel offset ($10 - 25\text{ px}$). The controller executes up to 6 iterations of closed-loop visual fine-centering: 

# Multi-Iteration Closed-Loop Visual Servoing (lawnmower_qr_mission.py)
for iteration in range(6):
    drone.poll()
    rclpy.spin_once(self.node, timeout_sec=0.03)
    frame = get_latest_camera_frame()
    
    ok, pts = self.detector.detect(frame)
    if ok and pts is not None:
        u, v = np.mean(pts[:, 0]), np.mean(pts[:, 1])
        err_px = math.hypot(u - cx, v - cy)
        
        # Convergence Check: Exit early if centroid is centered within tolerance
        if err_px <= self.args.center_tol:   # default: 25.0 px (approx 30 cm)
            print(f"[CENTERING] Lock acquired! Centroid error {err_px:.1f}px <= tolerance.")
            break
            
        # Pinhole reprojection & coordinate rotation
        m_per_px = 2.0 * curr_alt * math.tan(hfov / 2.0) / img_w
        dx_b = -(v - cy) * m_per_px
        dy_b = (u - cx) * m_per_px
        dn = dx_b * math.cos(yaw) - dy_b * math.sin(yaw)
        de = dx_b * math.sin(yaw) + dy_b * math.cos(yaw)
        
        # Apply 0.75 proportional gain to eliminate remaining offset without overshoot
        target_qr_ned[0] += dn * 0.75
        target_qr_ned[1] += de * 0.75
        
        # Dwell 0.7s with active setpoint streaming to let dynamics settle
        t_settle = time.time()
        while time.time() - t_settle < 0.7:
            drone.poll()
            drone.setpoint(*target_qr_ned)
            time.sleep(0.04)

8. Complete 6-Stage Mission Finite State Machine (FSM)

Stage & State Primary Operations Safety & Transition Conditions
Stage 1: Takeoff in Blue Zone Switches to GUIDED mode, arms brushless motors, issues MAV_CMD_NAV_TAKEOFF to 4.0m altitude. Polls telemetry until $-Z_{\text{NED}} \ge 3.4\text{m}$ (85% of target). Dwells 1.5s to stabilize hover.
Stage 2: Memorize Initial QR Downward camera captures qr_initial on takeoff pad. Orders quad corners, extracts 21×21 module grid into RAM. Stores binary matrix $\mathbf{G}_{\text{ref}}$ and decoded text (AEROTHON_QR_02). Falls back to disk asset if occluded.
Stage 3: Transition to Green Zone Navigates from Blue Zone $(N=-25.0, E=0.0)$ across the safety buffer to Green Zone entry waypoint $(N=-16.0, E=-18.0)$. Interpolates setpoints at $1.5\text{ m/s}$ until distance to entry $\le 1.0\text{m}$. Vision matcher disabled to prevent false pad match.
Stage 4: Lawnmower Survey Executes serpentine sweep along East columns alternating North/South sweeps at $1.5\text{ m/s}$. Vision matcher active. Triggers on similarity $S \ge 0.88$ or payload match. Instantly commands targeted BRAKE mode.
Stage 5: Precision Fly-To & Visual Servoing Dwells 1.5s in BRAKE mode. Navigates to projected target $\mathbf{P}_{\text{target\_ned}}$. Runs multi-iteration fine visual servoing. Locks centroid within $\le 25\text{ px}$. Visual lock achieves sub-centimeter accuracy ($0.7\text{ px}$).
Stage 6: Holding Alignment Hover Maintains locked hover directly over matched QR for --align-duration (default: 10.0s). Streams setpoints at 20 Hz. Displays live countdown HUD. If --land-after is set, issues LAND; otherwise holds steady hover indefinitely.
